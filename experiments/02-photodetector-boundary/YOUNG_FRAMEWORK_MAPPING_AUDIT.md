# Young–Sarovar–Léonard Framework Mapping Audit — Experiment 02

**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Status:** detailed closest-framework mapping; broad microscopic-framework novelty rejected  
**Priority:** unresolved; no novelty claim

This file maps the Experiment-02 resource/process program against the closest direct photodetector framework currently identified:

```text
K. C. Young, M. Sarovar, and F. Léonard,
General modeling framework for quantum photodetectors,
Phys. Rev. A 98, 063835 (2018),
arXiv:1811.08018,
DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.98.063835.
```

and its companion fundamental-limit work:

```text
K. C. Young, M. Sarovar, and F. Léonard,
Fundamental limits to single-photon detection determined by quantum coherence and backaction,
Phys. Rev. A 97, 033836 (2018),
arXiv:1803.10558,
DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.97.033836.
```

The objective is to determine whether Experiment 02 has discovered a genuinely new physical detector framework or has instead assembled established physical and decision-theoretic layers around an already-established microscopic detector model.

---

## 1. Hard boundary established by Young et al.

Young–Sarovar–Léonard already treat a photodetector as a quantum dynamical physical system rather than merely a scalar responsivity/noise number.

Their program explicitly connects

```text
incident quantum optical field
-> microscopic detector dynamics
-> internal detector-state evolution
-> amplification / observable response
-> measurement/readout performance.
```

That is the same broad physical territory entered by the early Experiment-02 chain

```text
photon interaction
-> microscopic excitation
-> amplification / trapping
-> persistent record
-> readout.
```

### Claim correction

Experiment 02 must **not** claim novelty for introducing a general microscopic quantum-photodetector framework connecting light–matter interaction to detector response.

Status: **DIRECT PRIOR ART.**

---

## 2. Microscopic transduction is already central prior art

Experiment 02 separated

```text
photon absorption
```

from

```text
useful detector transduction / record formation.
```

Young et al. likewise model the physical detector degrees of freedom and the dynamics by which an optical excitation produces detector response.

Thus the broad statement

> `absorption alone is not the whole detector`

is physically correct but is not a new detector-modeling insight.

Experiment 02's semiconductor specialization

```text
absorption
-> e-h excitation
-> extraction/collection vs recombination
-> record/readout
```

should be interpreted as one concrete realization of an established detector-dynamics viewpoint.

---

## 3. Amplification / irreversible response is also established territory

Experiment 02 reinterpreted amplification as

```text
mapping a fragile microscopic distinction into a robust accessible output
```

rather than `creating` new information about photon arrival.

Young et al. explicitly include amplification / detector-response dynamics in their general modeling program.

Therefore the broad detector chain

```text
microscopic trigger
-> amplification
-> macroscopic readable response
```

is not a candidate novelty.

The Experiment-02 rate-matching equations may still be useful model-specific calculations, but the conceptual stage decomposition is established.

---

## 4. Quantum coherence and backaction limits are already direct photodetector prior art

The companion 2018 paper explicitly studies fundamental single-photon detector limits determined by quantum coherence and measurement backaction.

This blocks a broad novelty route based on statements such as

```text
photodetection performance is fundamentally limited by quantum coherence / backaction;
measurement need not be described purely by absorbed energy;
microscopic coherent dynamics can constrain detector efficiency/performance.
```

These are already direct photodetector research topics.

---

## 5. Where Experiment 02 genuinely goes beyond the *scope* of that microscopic model

Going beyond the scope of a paper is **not the same as being novel**.

Experiment 02 later added several layers that are not simply microscopic detector Hamiltonian dynamics:

```text
A. classical all-task decision ordering / Blackwell comparison;
B. quantum statistical/channel comparison;
C. explicit task priors, losses, nuisance timing, and waveform dependence;
D. phase/time reference resources and restricted operation sets;
E. spatial/parallel channel count and active-channel search;
F. adaptive stopping and controller/communication resources;
G. repeated-use catalyst correlations / channel memory;
H. one-shot resource tails rather than averages;
I. causal output geometry and precharged-energy accounting;
J. record export, source side information, and thermodynamic cycle closure.
```

However, A–J individually belong to established neighboring theories.

The fact that Young et al. do not make all of them central detector coordinates does **not** by itself create a new physical theorem when Experiment 02 collects them.

Current status:

```text
BROADER CROSS-LAYER SYNTHESIS
!=
PROVEN NEW PHYSICS.
```

## 7. Important asymmetry in the comparison

Young et al. start from a **physical detector model** and ask what detector behavior/performance follows.

The provisional Experiment-02 framework later starts one level higher:

```text
physical resource model R
-> allowed detector strategies/processes C_D(R)
-> task-specific optimum decision risk.
```

This broader abstraction can contain Young-type models as members of the achievable detector-process set.

But this inclusion relation is not itself new physics.

Mathematically it resembles standard resource-constrained decision/control optimization.

Therefore the only defensible research question is:

> **Does a specifically photodetector physical constraint on `R` produce a new nontrivial achievable-process boundary that is not already supplied by Young-type detector dynamics plus standard decision/resource theory?**

That remains OPEN.

All are already substantially occupied by Young–Sarovar–Léonard and related photodetection literature.

## 10. Possible surviving research route after Young mapping

A publishable contribution would now require at least one of the following:

```text
1. a new physical constraint on a real photodetector architecture;
2. a new cross-layer bound coupling microscopic dynamics to a decision/latency/resource target;
3. a new impossibility result that remains after cavities, long interaction time, external references, adaptive control, pumps, and side information are charged;
4. an experimentally useful design law not already derivable from standard detector + signal-processing theory;
5. a new measurable resource tradeoff validated in a real material/device class.
```

The broad framework alone is not enough.

> **Young–Sarovar–Léonard already occupy the core microscopic quantum-photodetector framework territory. Experiment 02's broader resource/process construction may be useful as a cross-layer synthesis, but it has not yet demonstrated new detector physics. The novelty burden has moved entirely onto specific physical constraints or cross-layer tradeoff theorems.**

Status: **AUDIT CONCLUSION / NO NOVELTY CLAIM.**
